## Common confusion — “forehead a sounds like mouth ā”

You’re not doing it wrong.

**`a` and `ā` are the same open mouth quality.** Difference is **duration**:

| | Sound | Length |
|--|-------|--------|
| **a** | open “uh” | short tap · 1 mātrā |
| **ā** | same quality | held · 2 mātrās |

Touching the forehead and **sustaining** attention stretches the chant into `ā`. Expected.

**Two jobs:**

| Layer | Locus | Sound |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Varṇa (mouth) | — | `a` short · `ā` long |
| Sthāna / nyāsa | **where you install** the phoneme on the body | still mouth `a` or `ā` |

Forehead is **not** where `a` is articulated. It is where you **deposit** `a`.

> **Mouth decides how it sounds. Locus decides where it is installed.**

**Drill:**

```text
touch forehead → short a (one beat)
touch mouth    → held  ā (two beats)
no touch:      a . ā ā . a . ā ā .
```

If forehead-`a` becomes `ā`, pull back to a short tap when you mean `a`.
