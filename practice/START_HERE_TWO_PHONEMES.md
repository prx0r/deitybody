## Where to begin (exact answer)

**Night 1 pair: अ a (forehead) + आ ā (mouth/face).**

Why this pair first:

| Reason | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Normal varṇamālā | First two phonemes — Stage I order, not Mālinī permutation yet |
| Easiest loci | Forehead + mouth/face = precise touch targets |
| PTv secondary | a = anuttara/cit · ā = ānanda (only after sound+touch stable) |
| Aham key | **a** is the emergence phoneme of a…ha…ṃ |
| Depth | 2 sounds done properly > 50 done vaguely |

Do **not** start with Mālinī’s `na`. Master ordinary order + touch first. Mālinī body install comes after the instrument is known.

## Why 2 per night works

Abhinavagupta does not give a modern week-1 curriculum. *Tantrāloka* presents:

```text
anupāya → śāmbhava → śākta → āṇava
```

(immediate recognition downward into concrete methods).

**Body practice travels the opposite way:**

```text
sound/breath/body → visualization → internal cognition → recognition
```

That is āṇava — designed for embodied practice. Not a bastardization.

**Mālinīvijayottara 2.21** defines āṇava-samāveśa with five components we are literally using:

| Component | Meaning | Your stage |
|-----------|---------|------------|
| **uccāra** | breath / utterance | later (V) |
| **karaṇa** | body instrument / posture | later (III) |
| **dhyāna** | visualization | later (IV) — after association stable |
| **varṇa** | phonemes | **NOW (I)** |
| **sthāna-prakalpanā** | placing loci | **NOW (II)** |

You are not “playing the alphabet and imagining body parts.” You are running **varṇa + sthāna** — two of five āṇava components — at beginner resolution.

## Night 1 — full card (~15 min)

### Setup
- Seat spine long, jaw soft  
- Breath natural — no forced ratios (TĀ 4.91)  
- Optional audio: `/root/sanskrithelp/public/audio/phonemes/`  
- No music/drone required  

### 1. Phonetics only (3 min)
Say cleanly, attention on **mouth and breath**:

```text
a … a … a …
ā … ā … ā …
```

Feel openness vs length. Do not touch yet.

### 2. Hear → touch → say (4 min)
For **a**:
1. Hear or say **a**  
2. Touch **forehead** lightly  
3. Say **a** again while touching  
4. Notice: sound + forehead together  

For **ā**:
1. Same with **mouth/face** locus  
2. Long open sound  

Alternate a / ā three times each.

### 3. Hear → feel without touch (3 min)
Say **a** — without touching, let forehead become salient.  
Say **ā** — mouth/face salient.

### 4. Internal generate (2 min)
Eyes soft. Internally produce **a** → forehead lights as locus (not fantasy — just attention).  
Then **ā** → mouth/face.

### 5. Glyph only if stable (1–2 min)
If 3–4 felt clear, add:

```text
अ  a  forehead
आ  ā  mouth/face
```

See glyph while speaking once each.

### 6. Log (15 sec)
`n1 | a+ā | locus clear? y/n | any tension`

## Common confusion — “forehead a sounds like mouth ā”

You’re not doing it wrong.

**`a` and `ā` are the same open mouth quality.** Difference is **duration**:

| | Sound | Length |
|--|-------|--------|
| **a** | open “uh” | short tap · 1 mātrā |
| **ā** | same quality | held · 2 mātrās |

Touching the forehead and **sustaining** attention stretches the chant into `ā`. Expected.

**Two jobs:**

| Layer | Locus | Sound |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Varṇa (mouth) | — | `a` short · `ā` long |
| Sthāna / nyāsa | **where you install** the phoneme on the body | still mouth `a` or `ā` |

Forehead is **not** where `a` is articulated. It is where you **deposit** `a`.

> **Mouth decides how it sounds. Locus decides where it is installed.**

**Drill:**

```text
touch forehead → short a (one beat)
touch mouth    → held  ā (two beats)
no touch:      a . ā ā . a . ā ā .
```

If forehead-`a` becomes `ā`, pull back to a short tap when you mean `a`.
