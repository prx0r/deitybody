How to Chant & Vibrate

Practical technique grounded in Tantrāloka, Mālinīvijayottaratantra, Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa, and Flood’s account of the tantric body. Not a frequency chart. Not medical. Your voice is the install.

The whole technique in one line

Chant a phoneme as a coordinated event: correct articulation in the mouth · breath carrying the sound · touch on the body locus · internal attention on that locus · later, the Devanāgarī glyph.

Do this with two phonemes a night, not fifty.

Source frame: Mālinīvijayottara 2.21 defines āṇava-samāveśa through uccāra (breath/utterance) · karaṇa (body instrument) · dhyāna (visualization) · varṇa (phonemes) · sthāna-prakalpanā (placing loci). Beginners start with varṇa + sthāna — sound and locus — not visualization.

1 · The technique — how to chant each phoneme

Step A — Articulation first (mouth)

Say the sound cleanly. Feel where it is made: throat, palate, tongue at teeth, lips. Sanskrit vargas already run back → front in the vocal tract:

PlaceConsonantsMouth sense
kaṇṭhyaक ख ग घ ङ ka kha ga gha ṅaback / velar
tālavyaच छ ज झ ञ ca cha ja jha ñapalate
mūrdhanyaट ठ ड ढ ण ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇatongue curl back
dantyaत थ द ध न ta tha da dha natongue at teeth
oṣṭhyaप फ ब भ म pa pha ba bha malips

Within each row: plain stop → +aspiration → voiced → voiced+aspiration → nasal. Example: ka plain · kha +breath burst · ga hum · gha hum+burst · ṅa nasal tunnel.

Step B — Breath carries the sound (uccāra)

Tantrāloka 4.91: prāṇāyāmo na kartavyaḥ śarīraṁ yena pīḍyate — do not practise prāṇāyāma in a way that torments the body.
  • Natural breath. The chant itself may lengthen the exhale — that is enough.
  • Do not force 1:4:2 while learning phonemes.
  • Say the sound on the out-breath, or with an easy inhale–exhale cycle.
  • Three clean repetitions beat one strained long note.

MV 2.21 uccāra = utterance / upward movement of sounded prāṇa. Later (Stage V) you train audible → subtle → mental. Now: clear voice, easy breath.

Step C — Touch + locus (nyāsa / sthāna)

While saying the phoneme, place your hand on its body locus. This is the basic meaning of nyāsa: putting, depositing, installing.

Mālinīvijayottara 3.36 — install Mālinī (bhinna-yoni, vowels/consonants mixed) for the sake of the Śakti-body (śākta-śarīra). Flood: the body is constructed through inscription, not discovered as anatomy.

Progression (do not skip):

  1. External touch — hand on locus while chanting.
  2. Touch then release — proprioceptive memory.
  3. Feel without touching — locus becomes salient from the sound alone.
  4. Internal generate — think the phoneme; locus appears.
  5. Glyph joins — see अ while hearing/feeling a.

Step D — What you are installing

Not “shoulder emits frequency ka.” You are reconstructing the body as the body of Śakti by installing the matrix from which articulated experience arises (Abhinavagupta / Flood entextualisation).

Common confusion — “forehead a sounds like mouth ā”

You are not doing it wrong. a and ā are the same open mouth quality. The difference is duration: short tap vs held. Touching a locus and sustaining attention will naturally stretch the chant into ā.
LayerLocus meansSound means
Stage I · Varṇa(mouth only for now)a short · ā long
Stage II · Sthāna / nyāsawhere you install the phoneme on the bodystill just mouth a or ā

Forehead is not where a is articulated. It is where you deposit a as a Mātṛkā nyāsa locus.

Mouth decides how it sounds. Locus decides where it is installed.
If forehead-a becomes ā, pull the sound back to a short tap when you mean a.

Drill

  1. Touch forehead → say short a (one beat).
  2. Touch mouth/face → say held ā (two beats).
  3. No touch: a . ā ā . a . ā ā . — keep length honest.

PTv power names (anuttara / ānanda) are secondary. Do not try to make “forehead a” sound mystical. Keep phonetics clean; nyāsa is installation, not a new vocal quality.

2 · What “vibrate” means here

Not this: “this letter vibrates the liver at 528 Hz.” There is no Hz-per-phoneme doctrine in this system.

3 · Night 1 — exact practice

Pair: अ a (forehead) + आ ā (mouth/face)

  1. Phonetics (3 min) — say a a a then ā ā ā. Mouth + breath only.
  2. Hear → touch → say (4 min) — touch forehead, say a; touch mouth/face, say ā. Three rounds each.
  3. Feel without touch (3 min) — sound alone should make the locus obvious.
  4. Internal (2 min) — think the phoneme; locus salient.
  5. Glyph if stable (1–2 min) — अ / आ with the sound.
  6. Log — one line: pair · clear? · tension?
Success tonight: saying a without looking makes forehead feel obvious — not fantasy, just where attention goes.

How to chant a short verse later (e.g. namaḥ śivāya)

  1. Aloud — clear articulation of each syllable.
  2. Micro-somatic — feel each consonant’s place (na dental · ma labial · ḥ visarga).
  3. Breath — whole phrase rides easy prāṇa.
  4. Hear yourself while speaking.
  5. Semantic field — namaḥ / śiva as more than dictionary glosses.
  6. Internal — silent repetition, then preverbal intention, then awareness.

You are watching language be born, then dissolve. Not: syllable 1 = organ, syllable 2 = organ…

4 · Six-stage path (where this sits)

StageNameFocus
IVarṇaPhonetics — start here, 2/night
IISthānaBody coordinates via touch — same nights
IIIKaraṇaBody reproduces map without touch
IVDhyānaGlyph / later visual qualities
VUccāraBreath-ride; audible → subtle → mental
VISaṃhāra / SṛṣṭiMap inward to awareness and back out

Abhinavagupta presents upāyas as anupāya → śāmbhava → śākta → āṇava. Body practice travels the opposite way — that is āṇava, designed for embodied method (Tantrasāra ch.5). Starting somatic is correct, not a distortion.

Six-stage order is a pedagogical reconstruction. Every component is grounded; the sequencing is modern training design.
